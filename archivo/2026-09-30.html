AI & Claude lesson: Tokens

A token is the small chunk of text a language model reads and writes. Models do not see letters or whole words. A tokenizer splits text into pieces, such as a whole common word, part of a rarer word, or punctuation, and maps each piece to a number. In English, one token is roughly three-quarters of a word, though this varies by language and content.

Example: the sentence "Payments are growing" may become four or five tokens, while a long technical term may split into several. The same idea in Spanish often uses more tokens than in English.

Why it matters: providers price usage per token, and every model has a limit on how many tokens it can handle at once. Shorter, clearer prompts cost less and leave room for longer answers.

Practical use in banking or strategy: before sending a 40-page credit policy to a model for summary, estimate its size in tokens. If it is too large, split it by section and summarize each part, then combine the summaries. Tracking tokens also lets you forecast the monthly cost of an automated reporting workflow.
