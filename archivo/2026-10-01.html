AI & Claude lesson: What is RAG?

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is a technique that lets a language model answer using documents it retrieves at the moment of the question, instead of relying only on what it learned in training. A system first searches a document collection for the passages most relevant to the question, then places those passages in the model's context window along with the question, and the model writes its answer from them.

Example: an employee asks, "What is the approval limit for unsecured loans?" The system finds the matching paragraph in the current credit policy manual and passes it to the model. The answer reflects the manual as it stands today, and the system can show which passage it used.

RAG does not retrain the model. When the policy changes, you update the document, not the model. Answer quality depends on retrieval quality: if the wrong passage is found, the answer can be wrong.

Practical use in banking and strategy: connect a model to approved sources such as credit policies, regulatory circulars or internal strategy memos, and require it to cite the passage behind each answer. This makes outputs checkable by compliance and keeps answers current without technical rework.
